Core Motivation

Detailed Problem Statement

The Challenge

Traditional input methods like physical mice and keyboards pose significant limitations in specialized environments. In sterile medical labs or operating theaters, touching hardware presents contamination risks. In high-traffic public kiosks, physical peripherals are prone to wear, tear, and hygiene concerns.

Our Objective

We aim to eliminate the "tactile barrier" by providing a high-fidelity gesture recognition system. By leveraging ubiquitous webcams, we remove the need for expensive depth sensors like Kinect, making non-contact interaction accessible and affordable.

Target Use-Cases

  • Sterile Environments: Surgery rooms and chemistry labs.
  • Interactive Kiosks: Public information hubs and ATMs.
  • Dynamic Presentations: Intuitive navigation for speakers.
  • Accessibility: Assisting users with limited motor control.

"The goal is a zero-latency, natural interface that feels like an extension of human intent rather than a struggle with hardware."

Academic Context

Literature Survey

The evolution of gesture-based systems has seen three primary phases. Our research addresses the limitations of sensitivity to noise and the high economic barriers of specialized hardware.

OpenCV Era

Relied on contour detection. Robustness was limited by environmental lighting and background clutter.

MediaPipe Pure

Excellent landmarking but prone to false positives when faces or multiple hands enter the frame.

Hardware Phase

Proprietary sensors (Leap/Kinect) offer depth but require high costs and complex external setup.
